DeepSeek SEO: a method for choosing trending keywords to publish fast

Choose trending keywords backed by real data: signal sources, grouping by intent, scoring topics and using AI to analyze without letting it invent numbers.

By Hữu Hùng · Published: 2026-04-26 · 7 min read

Quick answer: A trending keyword is only worth writing about when you have real data on demand and your ability to meet it. Take signals from real sources such as Search Console and Google Trends, use an AI model (such as DeepSeek) to group and score rather than letting it guess search volume, then pick only topics you can write better than others.

Why fast does not mean good

"Publishing fast on trends" is appealing, but trends come and go, and many sites chase the same topic. The winning article is usually the one with its own viewpoint or more reliable information, not just the one that arrives early. The method below helps you be fast in the topic-selection step without trading away quality.

Principle: AI analyzes, real data decides

A language model has no real search volume figures and can speak very confidently about things that are not real. So:

  • Numbers (search volume, trend, difficulty) come from keyword research tools and Search Console.
  • AI is used to group, find common ground, classify intent and suggest angles from the data you provide.

DeepSeek or any other model follows this principle in exactly the same way.

Step 1: Gather signals from several sources

  • Google Search Console: queries that have brought impressions to your site but have no page of their own, or rank low.
  • Google Trends: see a topic's trend over time and by region.
  • Your own customers and community: the questions they often ask, the recurring sticking points.
  • The keyword research tool you already use, for quantitative numbers.

Export the data to a file (CSV) to give to the AI for analysis.

Step 2: Group by search intent

Give the model the keyword list and ask it to group by intent:

  • Learning: "what is X", "how to do X".
  • Comparing: "how X and Y differ".
  • Doing: "guide to installing X", "setting up X".
  • Deciding to buy: "how much does X cost", "which X to buy".

Each group should be answered by its own article, not mixed together.

Step 3: Score the topics

For each group, score on four simple criteria (scale 1 to 5), decided by you rather than letting the AI score without basis:

  • Demand: based on the real numbers you have.
  • Relevance to your product or expertise.
  • Ability to write something different: do you have experience, data or a viewpoint of your own.
  • Effort: how much it costs to write this topic well.

Prioritize topics with high demand and relevance where you can write something different.

Step 4: Check competitors before writing

Look at the pages that currently rank for the topic. Ask: have they answered fully, is the information old or wrong, what can you add. If you have nothing to add, drop that topic.

Step 5: Make a brief, then write

For each chosen topic, create a brief: search intent, the questions to answer, the point of difference, reference sources, internal links. Only then write, see Claude Code SEO Agent: a workflow for building SEO content with AI.

Common mistakes

  • Chasing a keyword because you heard it is "trending" with no numbers.
  • Asking the AI "which keywords have high search volume" and trusting the answer.
  • Writing many articles with the same idea to "cover" a keyword, leading to pages competing with each other and thin content, see An AI SEO system with Claude: automate content but still edit it.
  • Skipping updates to articles when the trend changes.

Measure the results

After publishing, track in Search Console: is the article indexed, and what are impressions and average position after a few weeks. This data feeds the next round of topic selection.
