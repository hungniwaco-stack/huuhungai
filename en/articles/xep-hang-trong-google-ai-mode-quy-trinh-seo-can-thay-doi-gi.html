Ranking in Google AI Mode: what does the SEO process need to change?

Per Google's docs, appearing in AI Overviews and AI Mode needs no special technique. A summary of the conditions, how to measure and the SEO process to keep.

By Hữu Hùng · Published: 2026-04-28 · 7 min read

Quick answer: According to Google, to appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode, a page only needs to be indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet. Google says there are no extra requirements, no special optimization needed and no special structured data. The SEO process therefore stays largely the same, with the focus on content that has its own value and a sound technical base.

What Google says

Google Search Central's "AI features and your website" documentation says:

  • A page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Google Search with a snippet. Beyond Search's standard technical requirements, there are no additional requirements.
  • No special optimization is needed, no new text file for AI, and no special schema.org structured data type to add.
  • Meeting all the requirements also does not guarantee that a page will be crawled, indexed or shown.

Google also has a separate guide to optimizing for generative AI features in Search. It advises focusing on unique, expert-led content and keeping a clear technical structure. Google also says that for Google Search you can ignore tactics such as chopping content into small pieces or creating an llms.txt file.

What this means for the SEO process

Since there is no separate "AI trick", a good process is still a good SEO process:

  1. Make sure pages can be crawled and indexed: not blocked in robots.txt or at the delivery network, with internal links so bots can find them.
  2. Useful, unique content: answer the search intent exactly, with something the reader cannot find elsewhere (real experience, your own data, well-founded opinion).
  3. Good page experience: fast to load, readable on phones, important content present as text.
  4. Structured data that matches the displayed content: do not mark up what readers cannot see.
  5. Quality images and video where appropriate.
  6. Verify the site in Search Console to catch technical errors.

Google also reminds you to keep Merchant Center and Business Profile information up to date if you sell goods or have a business location.

How to measure results

According to the AI features documentation, traffic from AI Overviews and AI Mode is counted in total search traffic in Search Console and sits in the Performance report under the "Web" search type. Google's optimization guide also recommends the Generative AI performance report in Search Console to see how your content appears in generative AI features. According to the help page, this report shows impressions over time and by page, device and country. If you do not see the report, it is usually because the site does not yet have enough impressions in these features.

Controlling how your content appears

Google lists the controls available to site owners:

  • robots.txt for Googlebot to control crawling.
  • nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet and noindex to limit how much content is displayed.
  • Google-Extended to limit the use of content for AI training and some other Google systems.

If content still appears after you add a limit, use the URL Inspection tool to confirm Googlebot sees the directive, then wait for a recrawl.

What really needs to change

  • Drop the idea of "optimizing for AI" as a separate trade. Put your effort into content quality and technical basics.
  • Do not create content in bulk just to take over results. Google's spam policy targets creating many pages only to manipulate rankings, including when AI is used, see An AI SEO system with Claude: automate content but still edit it.
  • Measure with your own data: Search Console instead of rumors about the algorithm.

About other platforms

The points above only concern Google Search. Other AI services may weigh other signals. If you are targeting them, follow each platform's guidance and do not extrapolate from Google.
