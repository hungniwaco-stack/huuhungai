QUICK: One prompt gives only a draft, not a finished page. For a good draft, provide real information (product, audience, desired action) and ask for a clear structure. After that you must check: no invented numbers, one `h1`, its own title and description, and structured data that matches the content.
## What one prompt can and cannot do
A model such as DeepSeek can quickly generate a draft page with a title, sections and a call to action. But it does not know what your product is really like, does not have your customer data and can invent things that sound plausible. So "one prompt" is only a starting point. The real value lies in the information you provide and in the checking afterward.
## Prepare real information first
Write down what only you know:
- **The product or service:** what it does, for whom, how it differs.
- **The audience and their specific pain points.**
- **The single action** you want visitors to take (sign up, call, buy).
- **Real evidence:** figures, testimonials and logos you actually have the right to use. If you have none yet, say so clearly.
- **What must not be said:** claims you cannot prove.
## A structured prompt template
```
Write a draft landing page in English for: [name and description of the real product].
Audience: [description]. Desired action: [one action].
Structure: one short main title (h1), one description sentence, an action button,
three specific benefits, a section answering common questions, a closing call to action.
Rules: use only the information I provide. Do not invent numbers, testimonials,
customer names or percentages. Wherever a fact is missing, write [NEEDS INPUT].
Return clean HTML with heading tags in the order h1, h2.
```
The key points are the "do not invent" rule and the [NEEDS INPUT] placeholder, so that missing parts show up instead of being filled with fake information.
## The structure of a good landing page
- **Opening section:** a title that states the value clearly, one explanatory sentence and the main action button, visible immediately without scrolling.
- **Problem and solution:** the customer's pain and how the product solves it.
- **Specific benefits:** one clear idea per benefit, avoiding stock phrases.
- **Real evidence:** only what you have.
- **Questions answered:** answer the common objections.
- **A closing call to action.**
Few words in the right places are usually more effective than many words.
## A checklist after the AI makes the draft
**Content:**
- Is every claim true? Delete any figure, testimonial or customer name that you do not really have.
- Does the tone suit the brand, and is it free of stock phrases?
- Are the benefits specific or just flowery language?
**SEO technical:**
- Exactly one `h1`, with `h2`s in order and no skipped levels.
- A page title and meta description unique to this page, not duplicating another page.
- A canonical tag.
- Structured data (if used) matches the content displayed on the page.
**Experience:**
- The action button is clear and easy to tap on a phone.
- The page loads fast, the text is large enough and the contrast is good.
- The form collects only the necessary information and there is a privacy policy.
## Do not reuse the same template for many pages
If you create many landing pages with the same prompt and only change the product name, the results will be so alike that they become thin content. ## Suggested workflow
1. Prepare the real information.
2. Run the prompt to get a draft.
3. Edit by hand: remove invented parts, add facts, adjust the tone.
4. Check against the list above.
5. Publish, then measure results with real data and improve step by step.
